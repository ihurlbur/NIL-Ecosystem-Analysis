# NIL-Ecosystem-Analysis

Strategic analysis of the evolving Name, Image, and Likeness (NIL) ecosystem, examining how athletes, universities, brands, agencies, technology platforms, and collectives interact within a rapidly changing marketplace. The project evaluates the competitive strategies shaping athlete representation and sponsorship and identifies potential strategic moves for both established and emerging players. This was a group project as part of USC Marshall's "DSO 556 - Business Models for Digital Platforms" course.

## Overview

This project examines the athlete representation and NIL sponsorship marketplace as a multi-sided platform ecosystem.

The expansion of NIL created a significantly larger and more complex market for athlete sponsorship and representation, introducing new participants and business models alongside traditional sports agencies. The analysis evaluates how value is created and captured across the ecosystem and how different organizations are competing to establish defensible positions as the market develops.

The analysis focuses on:

- The roles of athletes, universities and teams, brands and donors, and ecosystem orchestrators
- How traditional sports agencies are adapting their business models to the NIL market
- How technology platforms and NIL collectives compete by serving previously underserved segments of the athlete market
- The importance of network effects, platform liquidity, multi-homing, and switching costs
- Strategic moves that could shape the next phase of athlete representation and NIL sponsorship

## Key Findings

- The NIL marketplace functions as a multi-sided ecosystem connecting athletes, universities, brands, donors, agencies, technology platforms, and collectives.
- Traditional sports agencies are expanding beyond representation through consolidation, vertical integration, data capabilities, and broader platform-style service offerings.
- Technology-enabled NIL platforms can economically serve the majority of college athletes by automating athlete-brand matching and reducing transaction costs.
- University-affiliated NIL collectives create strong localized networks by concentrating athletes, donors, and commercial activity around individual institutions.
- The ecosystem continues to face fragmentation and multi-homing, limiting the ability of any single platform to establish dominant network effects.
- Data and analytics could become an increasingly important competitive advantage as organizations develop better methods for valuing athletes, matching sponsors, and measuring sponsorship performance.
- Long-term competitive advantage will depend on which organizations can create the strongest connections between athletes, brands, universities, and supporting services while reducing friction across the ecosystem.

## Objectives

- Map the major participants and relationships within the athlete representation and NIL sponsorship ecosystem
- Evaluate the current health and maturity of the NIL marketplace using platform ecosystem frameworks
- Analyze the strategies used by established sports agencies to protect and expand their market positions
- Examine how technology platforms and NIL collectives have created alternative models for athlete-brand engagement
- Apply concepts including network effects, multi-homing, platform liquidity, switching costs, network clustering, and data flywheels to the NIL market
- Identify potential strategic moves for both incumbent agencies and emerging NIL platforms
- Evaluate how the structure of the ecosystem may evolve as athlete representation, sponsorship, technology, and university athletics continue to converge
